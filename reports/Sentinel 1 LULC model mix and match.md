# Fix the labels and measurement, not the encoder

**Labels, the radar and measurement noise limit the model, not its architecture. The best "mix and match" changes are therefore cheap: post-processing at inference, label handling and Sentinel-1 timing features. Encoder, decoder or foundation-model swaps rank lower.** Our own runs show this from several sides. Train mIoU stops at 0.85-0.88 while val mIoU peaks at 0.732, and that is the signature of label noise, not of a network that is too small. Roughly half of the remaining disagreement sits in a 20 m band along class boundaries, which Sentinel-1 cannot resolve. Most large confusion blocks are pixels whose radar looks like the predicted class. The literature points the same way. On the closest analogue, a SAR-only land-cover model trained on noisy optical-derived labels, every loss or ensemble tweak added only 0.2-0.8 mIoU points. SAR-pretrained encoders add about 2 points over supervised pretraining on Sentinel-1 segmentation. Learned temporal encoders are expected to add 0-3 points over a model like ours, which already sees the whole year through its statistics. Our runs repeat with about ±0.02 mIoU of noise, which is as large as almost every expected gain. The most important change to the three 3rd-iteration notebooks is therefore statistical. They need 3 seeds for screening and 5 for the reference and any finalist, and one shared reference pool instead of three duplicate ones. The encoder sweep should stay, because it is the course's "comparison of model variants", but it should be expected to come out null. Label smoothing 0.2 has negative remote-sensing evidence and should be swapped for a smoothing-0 control. The real new upside comes from additions: a zero-training inference layer, one round of self-training, and a small set of S1-only timing channels. These are seed ensembling, 12-month softmax averaging, flip TTA, and per-class bias tuning that targets Water's precision of 0.59. Realistically, all of this moves rural test mIoU from 0.679 to about 0.70-0.72, with the clearest gains in Water, Snow & ice and Crop/Grass.

## The model is label-bound, and our own runs prove it

Since the first iteration, the measured gains have all come from data and input changes, not from architecture, which has never changed from a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder. Adding yearly temporal context lifted val mIoU by 0.09-0.12. Moving from a 15-epoch to a 40-epoch schedule lifted rural test mIoU by **+0.040 to +0.055** (0.624/0.639 to 0.679). The second-iteration redesign cut the train-val gap from 0.18-0.21 to 0.12. That redesign added rural sites, gamma0 terrain flattening and labels on all pixels ([Black Forest report §11](2026-10-31%20Prototype%20test%201%20Black%20Forest.md); [own run analysis](../research_notes/Sentinel%201%20LULC%20model%20mix%20and%20match/own_run_analysis.md)). The learning curve of the current model, `Iter2_Test_1_Full`, rises fast to 0.70 val mIoU by epoch 8 and peaks at **0.732 at epoch 22**. After that, val loss rises from 0.530 to 0.568 while **train mIoU reaches only 0.854-0.885**. A network short of capacity would plateau with train and val low together. This network instead overfits labels that it still cannot reproduce. That fits the label statistics: WorldCover and Dynamic World agree on only **73.6 %** of training pixels, and on **55 % of Grassland** pixels ([Black Forest report §10](2026-10-31%20Prototype%20test%201%20Black%20Forest.md)). The gap between rural mIoU on agreement pixels (0.679) and on all WorldCover pixels (0.597) is a second measure of the same label ceiling.

The pixel-level analysis of the three logged prototype runs (Berlin and two Thane windows, all urban and scored against Esri 2021) shows where the errors sit. In Thane, **46-47 % of all errors lie within 20 m of a reference boundary**. That band covers only 11-12 % of the area, and the error rate falls to 2-3 % deeper than 11 pixels inside a patch. Sentinel-1 GRD resolves about 20 x 22 m, so a one- to two-pixel edge band is a sensor and label-geometry limit that no encoder can remove. The large confusion blocks have the same character. Examples are Esri Water at creek edges called Trees, Esri Crop called Trees, and Berlin street canopy that Esri counts as built. Their annual radar statistics sit closest to the class the model predicted: Water-to-Trees pixels average VV −7.5 dB, about 9-10 dB brighter than real water. The model follows the radar there. The one exception matters for the recommendations. **Crop/Grassland errors are partly radar-separable through VH temporal variability.** Crop pixels misread as Grassland have a VH standard deviation of 2.7-2.9 dB, between crop (3.3-3.6) and grassland (2.2), and their nearest class centroid is Crop for 48-57 % of them ([own run analysis §1](../research_notes/Sentinel%201%20LULC%20model%20mix%20and%20match/own_run_analysis.md)). That is the only measured hint that a better use of the time series could recover pixels the current input wastes. These prototype windows are urban and scored against Esri, not against the WorldCover training labels, so they indicate the pattern rather than prove it for rural land.

The per-class rural scores locate the remaining upside. **Water has an IoU of 0.49 with precision 0.59 below recall 0.74**, and 23 % of predicted water is really Trees. **Snow & ice has an IoU of 0.45**, with 24 % of snow predicted as Bare and 39 % of predicted snow actually Bare. **Crop fields score 0.66, with 21 % predicted as Grassland**. Africa (0.49) and Oceania (0.44) are the weakest regions ([Iter2_Test_1_Full](../Weights/Iter2_Test_1_Full.zip); [Black Forest report §11.2](2026-10-31%20Prototype%20test%201%20Black%20Forest.md)). The literature agrees that this kind of plateau is not a hard wall. On DFC2020, U-Nets trained on MODIS labels that were only 37.2 % accurate reached 48.1 % against hand labels ([Schmitt et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.08254)). Our scores against WorldCover, which is itself only **76.7 % accurate** ([ESA WorldCover 2021](https://worldcover2021.esa.int)), therefore measure agreement with a consensus label, not truth.

## A ±0.02 noise floor decides which comparisons are readable

Two accidental identical repeats are the only noise measurement for the second iteration. Val mIoU moved by up to **0.018** (month_year: 0.741 to 0.723) and rural test by 0.015. The other two variants moved by 0.004 or less. In the first iteration, test mIoU differed by 0.02-0.06 between identical runs, and single-class IoU swung by up to 0.24, because Water and Snow each rest on one or two test sites ([own run analysis §2](../research_notes/Sentinel%201%20LULC%20model%20mix%20and%20match/own_run_analysis.md)). Validation has also misled once already. In Test_6, val ranked three variants within 0.014 of each other, and the variant it chose was the worst of them on test ([Test run 6 results](Test%20run%206%20results.md)).

If a single run carries a standard deviation of about 0.01 mIoU, the standard error of a difference between two configuration means is 0.01·√(2/n) for n seeds each. That is 0.010 with the planned 2 seeds, 0.008 with 3 and 0.006 with 5. This σ ≈ 0.01 is a conservative reading of the 0.004-0.018 repeat differences. The notebooks' rule, "beat the reference by more than 0.02 on mean val mIoU over 2 seeds", is about a two-standard-error test. It rarely calls a false winner, but it has little power. A true gain of 0.02 needs about **4-5 seeds per arm** for an 80 % chance of detection, and a true 0.01 gain needs about 16, which is out of reach. Almost every change in this report is expected to add 0.005-0.02. The plan should therefore accept openly that most single swaps will come out "not distinguishable from the reference", and spend seeds where they buy the most.

The cheapest seed saving is already in the notebooks. **E0, T0 and L0 are the same configuration with the same seeds (42 and 7)**, so the plan trains the reference six times to get two distinct samples. If training is deterministic, the three copies are identical. If it is not, they form a hidden noise sample. The better use of those runs is one shared reference pool of 5 seeds (42, 7, 1, 2, 3) under the ResNet-34 settings, reused by all three notebooks, with 3 seeds for every other configuration. Any configuration whose 3-seed mean beats the reference by more than 0.01 then gets 2 more seeds before a verdict. Using the same seed list in every arm also makes paired per-seed differences more informative within the training and label notebooks, where architecture and initialisation stay fixed. Two cheap reporting additions make the exam's critical discussion stronger. The first is a site-level bootstrap interval on rural test mIoU, since the test set has only 13 rural sites. The second is the mean over the last few EMA checkpoints alongside the best-epoch val score, because the maximum over epochs is biased upwards.

### Rank 2: inference-time mixing is the cheapest real gain

Ensembling and TTA need no new training. The notebooks already produce what they need: the shared reference pool alone yields five ResNet-34 models. On the DFC2025 SAR land-cover track, ensembling added **+0.61 mIoU** over the best single model ([Liu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13458)). Our own data shows the mechanism works here too. Averaging softmax over the neighbouring months already cut flicker from 2.4 % to 0.9 % at no cost in mIoU ([Iter2_Test_1_Full](../Weights/Iter2_Test_1_Full.zip)). Because the labels are annual, averaging all 12 monthly softmaxes gives an annual-consensus map that matches the target directly. That map is worth scoring next to the monthly one. The year_stats variant, with no monthly channels at all, already scored rural 0.646 against month_year's 0.639 in DEV ([Black Forest report §10](2026-10-31%20Prototype%20test%201%20Black%20Forest.md)). That suggests the target-month channel adds little to the annual label.

Flip TTA is inconsistent in the literature, from slightly negative to +1.6 % ([arXiv 2603.14132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14132)). Accept it only if it helps on val, and undo each flip on the logits before averaging ([Albumentations D4 TTA](https://albumentations.ai/docs/examples/example-tta-d4-aerial-segmentation/)). The most targeted zero-cost step is a per-class logit bias tuned on val. Water's precision of 0.59 below its recall of 0.74 means its decision threshold is too lenient, and for IoU the best operating point usually lies near precision ≈ recall. Snow shows the same imbalance on a smaller scale (0.60 vs 0.64). No source quantifies post-hoc bias tuning for SAR segmentation, so the estimate of +2 to +5 Water IoU is an inference from the precision-recall gap ([weak-classes notes](../research_notes/Sentinel%201%20LULC%20model%20mix%20and%20match/weak_classes_and_generalisation.md)). A seed ensemble does conflict with the earlier decision to ship "one final model". The bias and 12-month averaging steps do not, and the prototype can keep a single weight file.

### Ranks 3-6: label handling and loss changes are small but well targeted

Direct evidence for noise-robust losses on real map labels is thin, and the measured gains are small. The DFC2025 SAR winner gained **+0.19 from symmetric cross-entropy, +0.29 from Lovász and +0.80 from replacing labels with self-trained pseudo-labels** ([Liu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13458)). Self-training has the best evidence for its cost. Here that means taking the reference model's predictions on disagreement pixels where confidence exceeds about 0.9 and the prediction matches one of the two products, then retraining. It uses the teacher runs the reference pool already produces. Uniform label smoothing has **negative** remote-sensing evidence. In CromSS, which trained on Dynamic World and OSM labels, uniform smoothing lowered DFC2020 and Dynamic World scores by **0.3-2.4 mIoU** ([CromSS](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01217v3)). Planned run L3 (smoothing 0.2) therefore tests a direction already expected to hurt. A smoothing-0 run is more useful, because it checks whether the 0.1 already inside every model earns its place.

CromSS also found that confidence-based sample selection suppressed boundaries and fine detail ([CromSS](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01217v3)). That argues for L4's soft bootstrapping over hard small-loss filtering. It also argues for keeping disagreement pixels in training, as L0 does, rather than dropping them, as L1 does. ADELE-style early-learning correction warns that gains can be "marginal or even negative" when the noise is structured, as WorldCover/Dynamic World noise is ([ADELE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03740)). Soft targets from Dynamic World class probabilities are plausible, but they need a new GEE export. Even stored as 7 remapped uint8 bands, they add about 1 GB, which leaves almost no room under the 5 GB budget. Dynamic World and Esri also share a training reference, so their errors are correlated ([Venter et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/16/4101/html)). That makes a third-product vote less independent than it looks.

On the loss side, the evidence says not to raise rare-class weights further. Water and Snow already over-predict. Rare-class weighting and recall-biased Tversky losses push recall up and precision down, the wrong direction for both classes. The pipeline also counts rarity twice, through square-root median-frequency weights and a weighted sampler. A capped-weight run (T3) is the targeted test. On Sentinel-1 water, focal-Tversky beat edge-weighted CE by only +1.9 IoU and gave badly calibrated probabilities ([arXiv 2302.08180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08180)). Boundary, class-balanced, OHEM and SCE losses all have expected effects below 0.5 points or a risk of amplifying label noise.

### Rank 4: Sentinel-1 timing channels fix what the statistics miss

The nine yearly statistics are order-free. They tell the model how much backscatter varies, but not when. Our own history shows timing matters for Snow: the calendar-tied PCA variant scored snow IoU 0.813 against 0.529 without it, but it generalised badly to unseen sites and hemispheres ([Test run 6 results](Test%20run%206%20results.md)). Wet snow is the textbook S1 signal. Backscatter drops by about 2-3 dB below a snow-free reference. Fixed thresholds transfer badly: a −2 dB threshold gave only 32-44 % OA where an optimised threshold gave about 80 %. Networks fed the continuous difference image beat thresholds ([Gong et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/19/3575)).

A "minimum month minus snow-free-season median" channel stays strictly S1-only. So does the month of that minimum, encoded as sine and cosine and shifted by 6 months in the Southern Hemisphere. Together they give the model relative timing without tying it to the calendar. For Crop/Grass, S1 harmonic features match raw time series in accuracy ([INRAE hal-04823571](https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/RESEAU-TELEDETECTION-INRAE/hal-04823571v1)). A VH first-harmonic amplitude and phase therefore compresses the crop calendar into two channels, and it addresses exactly the VH-variability signal our separability test found. For Water, a temporal-persistence rule cut false positives by about **50 %** against Otsu thresholding ([EGU21-6982](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-6982.html)). An annual VV maximum, or VV's coefficient of variation in linear power, is the cheap analogue that separates permanently dark sand or tarmac from water. The layover/shadow mask is computed in preprocessing and then discarded. It belongs in the loss as an ignore mask and in the metric as a no-prediction mask, which keeps the DEM out of the model input. Check first that the shadow positions can be recovered from the stored NaN fill.

All these channels can be computed on the fly from the stored monthly stack, so they cost no disk space. None has a published CNN ablation, so the class-level gains of +2 to +5 IoU are reasoned estimates. A single run each, with 3 seeds, settles them. They are worth more than any encoder swap, because they add information the network does not currently have.

### Ranks 7-8: encoders, pretraining and temporal models stay on the plan, at lower expectations

Decoder swaps are second-order. In the one multi-architecture Sentinel-1 land-cover study, the top four models finished within **0.6 OA points** of each other, and DeepLabV3+ and PSPNet trailed by about 1-1.4 ([arXiv 1912.05067](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.05067)). Keep the U-Net decoder. Encoder swaps should land at a similar scale, and our learning curve argues against more capacity. E2 (ResNet-50 with ImageNet weights) is the least supported option on its own, because the model already overfits 2218 patches.

E2 earns its place as half of a clean pretraining comparison. TorchGeo ships five 2-channel Sentinel-1 ResNet-50 weights but no S1 ResNet-18 or ResNet-34 ([TorchGeo models](https://docs.torchgeo.org/en/stable/api/models.html)). SoftCon RN50 is the strongest of them on SAR linear probing, ahead of SSL4EO MoCo by +2.3 mAP on BigEarthNet-SAR and +4.7 accuracy on EuroSAT-SAR ([SoftCon](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20462v2)). On Sentinel-1 segmentation (DFC2020-S1), SAR-pretrained ViT-B models beat a supervised ViT-B by only **1.6-2.0 mIoU** and random initialisation by about 7 ([Copernicus-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11849)). Our weights are expected to gain less than that. They expect 2 channels of sigma0, while our input is 12 channels of terrain-flattened gamma0, so the first convolution must be expanded. An "E6: ResNet-50 + SoftCon-S1, conv1 expanded" run next to E2 isolates the pretraining effect at equal architecture. That is a good question for a CNN course. Expect −0.01 to +0.015.

E5 (no pretraining) answers the companion question cheaply. The PANGAEA benchmark found a plain U-Net from scratch among the top two on 6 of 11 datasets ([PANGAEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204v2)), so a small loss is a plausible result. E1 (VGG16-BN) and E4 (EfficientNet-B0) answer "do residual connections or size matter?" at almost no cost. E3 (DenseNet-121) is the most expendable. Prithvi has no Sentinel-1 input at all ([Prithvi-EO-2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02732v1)). The ViT foundation models (CROMA, TerraMind, Galileo, SMARTIES) fall outside a CNN course and have weak S1-only segmentation evidence.

For temporal models, learned attention does lead on Sentinel-2 benchmarks. On PASTIS, TSViT averages 65.4 mIoU against U-TAE's 63.1, and a well-sized 3D U-Net matches TSViT on one of two crop datasets ([TSViT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04944); [arXiv 2412.01944](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01944)). The big published jumps compare one date with the whole year, though, and our model already sees the year. Seasonal Sentinel-1 composites even beat dense series in one three-region study ([Russo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07230v1)). Our own 18-channel three_month_year variant did not beat the 12-channel month_year either. The cheapest gate is a 36-channel early-fusion U-Net (12 months x VV, VH, VV−VH), which changes only conv1 and costs one ordinary run. Only if it, or the timing channels, beats the reference is a small 3D-CNN or U-TAE worth 3-5 hours of code. U-TAE needs GroupNorm, because BatchNorm collapsed PASTIS mIoU from 63.1 to 36.0 ([U-TAE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.07933)). TSViT is built for 24x24-pixel crops, so it does not fit 256x256 tiles.

The regional gap is a data and label problem rather than an architecture one. A Europe-trained model drops from 0.705 to 0.424 OA on Africa ([SEN12MS study](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10351)). An auxiliary head that predicts noisy lat/lon added **+2.7 to +4.2 mIoU** across French domains without changing the input ([GeoMultiTaskNet](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.07750)). The remaining ~1.3 GB of data budget is best spent on more validation sites and on Africa/Oceania sites in a 4th iteration. Adding data now would invalidate every 3rd-iteration comparison against `Iter2_Test_1_Full`.

## What changes in the three notebooks

The notebooks are sound in design: one change at a time, selection on val only, and both agreement and all-pixel scores. The changes below keep that design. They reallocate runs and add a few targeted configurations.

This plan adds up to about 50 training runs. That is roughly 5-6 hours on the A6000 for the three existing notebooks plus about 1.5 hours for the features notebook, compared with 28 runs (about 2.5-3 hours) in the current plan. The time goes to seeds, which the current plan lacks.

The order should change slightly. Run the 5-seed reference and the zero-training inference layer first, because every later verdict depends on them. The encoder winner rule should require beating the reference by more than 0.02 on a 5-seed mean, not a 2-seed one, before `ENCODER` changes in the other notebooks. The likely outcome is that ResNet-34 stays. That outcome does not mean the sweep failed: it is the evidence for the exam's critical discussion that labels and radar, not the CNN, set the limit. When reporting L1 and L2, read both scores. L2 may lose on agreement pixels but win on all-pixel mIoU, and the main score is biased towards easy, homogeneous pixels.

## Conclusion

Most of the planned "mix and match" work tests capacity, the one factor our evidence clears. The upside lies at the two ends of the pipeline instead. At the input end, S1 timing channels give the model information it lacks. At the output end, ensembles, annual softmax averaging and per-class bias tuning stop it over-predicting rare classes. Between them, label handling decides what the network is asked to copy. A combined gain of about 0.02-0.04 rural mIoU is realistic. Any single swap that claims more than 0.03 should be suspected of seed luck until 5 seeds confirm it.

The more lasting change concerns method. With a ±0.02 noise floor, 21 validation sites and 13 rural test sites, the project measures differences of a few hundredths no better than coarsely. That makes seed counts and site-bootstrap intervals part of the result rather than housekeeping. For an exam that rewards critical discussion, a well-powered null result on the encoders is worth as much as a new headline number. That null result needs a clear attribution of the remaining error to boundary bands, label disagreement and radar-indistinguishable pixels.
